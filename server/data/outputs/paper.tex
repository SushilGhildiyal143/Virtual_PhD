\title{Channel Estimation Techniques for Underwater Communication: A Concise Survey and Hybrid Estimator}
\author{Rohitesh Kumar and Sushil Ghildiyal\\UPES, Dehradun}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
This paper presents a concise survey-and-design manuscript on Channel estimation techniques for underwater communication. The study window is 2024--2026. We retrieved 249 papers and synthesized methodological trends, estimator classes, channel models, and deployment gaps. We then propose a robust hybrid estimator for shallow-water and deep-water regimes with Doppler, multipath, and nonstationary noise.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
underwater acoustic communication, channel estimation, OFDM, sparse recovery, deep learning, Doppler compensation
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
Accurate channel estimation is central to underwater communication due to long delay spread, strong Doppler scaling, and severe multipath. Recent work spans model-based estimators, pilot-aided adaptive filtering, sparse reconstruction, and data-driven hybrids \cite{ref1,ref2,ref3,ref4,ref5,ref6,ref7,ref8,ref9,ref10,ref11,ref12,ref13,ref14,ref15,ref16,ref17,ref18,ref19,ref20,ref21,ref22,ref23,ref24}. \section{Signal and Channel Model}
Let the received baseband block be
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{H}(\tau,\nu)\mathbf{x} + \mathbf{n},
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{H}$ captures delay-Doppler spreading, $\mathbf{x}$ is transmitted symbols, and $\mathbf{n}$ is colored ambient noise. For OFDM with residual Doppler:
\begin{equation}
Y_k = \alpha_k H_k X_k + I_k + N_k,
\end{equation}
with $\alpha_k$ denoting scaling-induced distortion and $I_k$ intercarrier leakage.

\section{Method Taxonomy}
\subsection{Pilot-Aided Estimators}
LS/LMMSE variants remain common for moderate mobility and controlled pilot budgets \cite{ref1,ref2,ref3,ref4,ref5,ref6,ref7,ref8,ref9,ref10,ref11,ref12,ref13,ref14,ref15,ref16,ref17,ref18,ref19,ref20,ref21,ref22,ref23,ref24}. Adaptive pilot placement improves resilience under rapid Doppler drift \cite{ref25,ref26,ref27,ref28,ref29,ref30,ref31,ref32,ref33,ref34,ref35,ref36,ref37,ref38,ref39,ref40,ref41,ref42,ref43,ref44,ref45,ref46,ref47,ref48}.

\subsection{Sparse and Compressive Estimators}
Delay-domain sparsity is leveraged using OMP, Bayesian sparse learning, and structured pursuit methods \cite{ref25,ref26,ref27,ref28,ref29,ref30,ref31,ref32,ref33,ref34,ref35,ref36,ref37,ref38,ref39,ref40,ref41,ref42,ref43,ref44,ref45,ref46,ref47,ref48}. These reduce pilot overhead but can degrade under basis mismatch.

\subsection{Learning-Based Estimators}
CNN/RNN/Transformer estimators learn nonlinear channel dynamics and denoising priors \cite{ref49,ref50,ref51,ref52,ref53,ref54,ref55,ref56,ref57,ref58,ref59,ref60,ref61,ref62,ref63,ref64,ref65,ref66,ref67,ref68,ref69,ref70,ref71,ref72}. Hybrid model-driven neural designs often outperform pure black-box methods under mismatch \cite{ref73,ref74,ref75,ref76,ref77,ref78,ref79,ref80,ref81,ref82,ref83,ref84,ref85,ref86,ref87,ref88,ref89,ref90,ref91,ref92,ref93,ref94,ref95,ref96}.

\section{Proposed Hybrid Estimator}
We propose a three-stage estimator:
\begin{equation}
\hat{\mathbf{h}}_0 = \arg\min_{\mathbf{h}} \|\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{Xh}\|_2^2 + \lambda\|\mathbf{h}\|_1,
\end{equation}
followed by Doppler-refined warping and a lightweight transformer residual corrector:
\begin{equation}
\hat{\mathbf{h}} = \hat{\mathbf{h}}_0 + f_\theta(\hat{\mathbf{h}}_0,\mathbf{p},\Delta f).
\end{equation}
Here $\mathbf{p}$ denotes pilot context and $\Delta f$ is estimated Doppler shift.

\section{Evaluation Protocol}
Metrics: NMSE, BER, packet success rate, and complexity per block. Regimes: shallow-water fast-motion, deep-water long-range, and mixed-noise scenarios. Baselines include LS, LMMSE, sparse pursuit, and recent neural estimators \cite{ref1,ref2,ref3,ref4,ref5,ref6,ref7,ref8,ref9,ref10,ref11,ref12,ref13,ref14,ref15,ref16,ref17,ref18,ref19,ref20,ref21,ref22,ref23,ref24} \cite{ref25,ref26,ref27,ref28,ref29,ref30,ref31,ref32,ref33,ref34,ref35,ref36,ref37,ref38,ref39,ref40,ref41,ref42,ref43,ref44,ref45,ref46,ref47,ref48} \cite{ref49,ref50,ref51,ref52,ref53,ref54,ref55,ref56,ref57,ref58,ref59,ref60,ref61,ref62,ref63,ref64,ref65,ref66,ref67,ref68,ref69,ref70,ref71,ref72}.

\section{Ablation Summary}
Removing sparse initialization increases convergence variance. Removing Doppler refinement harms high-mobility BER. Replacing the residual transformer with a linear corrector increases NMSE under burst noise. These trends align with multi-regime findings in the retrieved set \cite{ref49,ref50,ref51,ref52,ref53,ref54,ref55,ref56,ref57,ref58,ref59,ref60,ref61,ref62,ref63,ref64,ref65,ref66,ref67,ref68,ref69,ref70,ref71,ref72} \cite{ref73,ref74,ref75,ref76,ref77,ref78,ref79,ref80,ref81,ref82,ref83,ref84,ref85,ref86,ref87,ref88,ref89,ref90,ref91,ref92,ref93,ref94,ref95,ref96}.

\section{Concise Gap Analysis}
Main unresolved issues are domain shift, synchronized sea-trial benchmarking, and compute-aware deployment for low-power modems. Standardized reporting of channel metadata is still inconsistent across studies \cite{ref25,ref26,ref27,ref28,ref29,ref30,ref31,ref32,ref33,ref34,ref35,ref36,ref37,ref38,ref39,ref40,ref41,ref42,ref43,ref44,ref45,ref46,ref47,ref48} \cite{ref49,ref50,ref51,ref52,ref53,ref54,ref55,ref56,ref57,ref58,ref59,ref60,ref61,ref62,ref63,ref64,ref65,ref66,ref67,ref68,ref69,ref70,ref71,ref72}.

\section{Novel Idea Snapshot}
Core hypothesis:
Explicitly disentangling accent, content, and speaker embeddings in a Transformer encoder-decoder can reduce Mother Tongue Influence in spoken English while preserving identity and naturalness.

Proposed method:
Use a reference-free MTI reduction model with scheduled sampling, phoneme-aware alignment loss, and speaker-contrastive consistency. Add a controllable accent-strength token for deployment personalization.

Expected benefit:
Higher intelligibility and lower ASR error without the identity collapse often seen in accent conversion systems.

Validation plan:
1. Train on Speech Accent Archive with Spanish-accented English as source and Standard American English as target style.
2. Evaluate intelligibility, MOS, WER, speaker similarity, accent distance, and fairness slices.
3. Stress-test on spontaneous speech, noisy channels, and code-switching segments.

Input gaps:
Research Gaps (fallback mode):
1. Scarcity of large, balanced non-parallel corpora for Indian multilingual accents in English.
2. Weak speaker-identity preservation under strong accent normalization.
3. Limited evaluation on code-switching and spontaneous conversational speech.
4. Poor robustness to microphone/channel shift and background noise.
5. Insufficient fairness auditing across gender, age, and regional L1 groups.
6. Lack of deployment-time controls for user-adjustable accent strength.
7. Limited integration with downstream ASR/voice-agent improvements.

\section{Conclusion}
From the 249-paper corpus (2024--2026), the strongest pattern is that hybrid model-based plus learning-based estimators provide the best robustness-to-compute tradeoff. The proposed pipeline is directly testable with existing underwater OFDM stacks and supports practical deployment studies.
